# Review of the P4 bar charts, pie charts and nets plan

**Status: written 2026-10-07 by an independent reviewer agent (Claude) that did not write the plan.** This is a review, not a decision. Codex still has to record accept, modify, reject or defer for each finding. It does not approve any lesson content or curriculum placement. No human review has happened. Reviewed: `docs/p4-charts-nets-plan.md` and `docs/p4-charts-nets-evidence.md`. No application code, tests, lesson JSON or config were changed. Scratch scripts are outside the repo.

## Verdict

**PROCEED WITH THE CHANGES BELOW.** The mathematics of cube nets is correct (I reproduced every figure independently). The repo plumbing claims are largely right. Two things must be fixed before WP2, and a short list of test-plan and design numbers must be corrected before WP1. Nothing found justifies stopping the whole phase.

## A. Curriculum and evidence

Re-opened (summary tool, not raw pages): practicle P4 (matches: line graphs, pie charts, nets for prisms and pyramids; no bar graphs or percentages); practicle P5 ("find percentage in pie charts"); Learning Lab (pie charts and nets moved to P4, bar graphs P3); Odyssey nets (cube, cuboid, prism, pyramid; cone and cylinder only as 2D representations); Kiasu (bar graphs P3; line graphs and pie charts P4; nets P4; percentages P5 and not P3/P4); TLS Tutorials (pie charts and nets new at P4, no percentages); matrixmath (P4 pie charts and nets; P5 percentage); Wikipedia nets (11 nets of a cube; Dürer 1525; named nets 1543; no etymology). All eight cited URLs I checked return HTTP 200 (jimmymaths returns 301). Independent searches (my own queries, not the planner's) returned school documents listing pie charts in Term 3 and nets in Term 4 of P4 (Tao Nan, Admiralty, Tampines North, West Spring, Shuqun, Yuhua, Henry Park). I could not open any of them (all 404 through the fetch tool). The search snippet also gave syllabus-style wording: "identifying 2D representations of cubes, cuboids, cones, cylinders, prisms and pyramids", "drawing 2D representations", "identifying nets of 3D solids", and an isometric-grid item. That snippet is a search-tool summary, not a page I read.

- Level claims hold at the strength stated. The plan hedges well ("P4 candidate", "level-unconfirmed"). I found no overstatement of "most sources".
- Pie charts as fractions only: justified. Kiasu and TLS say percentages are P5 or unmentioned at P4. Including sector angles or percentages as learner answers at P4 is not supported. Offering them as a clearly optional later lesson is acceptable but should wait for the school's topic list. The plan does not under-deliver on percentages; it does under-deliver on reading real pie charts (R-81). The plan should record to the user that "bar charts" are P3 content per five sources.
- "Why is it called a net": honestly hedged. It says the one source does not explain the word, and marks the "Netz" remark as the planner's own knowledge. The child explanation is fine. I could not find a source that explains the word either.

## B. Mathematics, with my own scratch code

Scripts in `/tmp/claude-1000/-home-makodev58-aura-math/4e292714-a97a-4492-b213-4437224f7f2f/scratchpad/review/` (`nets.py`, `geo.py`, `comp.py`, `contrast.py`, `pie.py`).

- Fixed hexominoes 216, free 35: confirmed by growth enumeration with translation normalisation and 8 symmetries.
- Cube nets: 64 fixed orientations (rotations and mirror images both counted) and 11 free: confirmed with the plan's rolling algorithm, and confirmed again by a separate 3D fold using integer rotation matrices on face centres. The two methods agree on validity for all 216. So all 11 pass and all 24 other free hexominoes fail.
- Opposite faces: the rolling map (pairs 0-1, 2-3, 4-5) matches the geometric centroid-opposite test with zero mismatches over all 64 nets and all cell pairs. Starter net `A.../BCDE/..F.` gives A-F, B-D, C-E.
- Row profiles 1-4-1 (6), 2-3-1 (3), 2-2-2 (1), 3-3 (1): confirmed. Bounding boxes: ten of 3 by 4 (either way), one 2 by 5: confirmed.
- Starter nets: A (U shape) fails, B folds, C (five in a row) fails, D (2 by 2 block) fails: confirmed. Complete-the-net starter: a and d fold, b and c fail: confirmed.
- Completions: 45 distinct fixed five-square pieces, each with 8 to 11 empty neighbours, exactly 4 completions each: confirmed (not to be hard-coded, as the plan says). Only 8 of the 12 free pentominoes arise.
- Pie arithmetic: all six slice counts divide 360 (90, 72, 60, 45, 36, 30 degrees). Every lesson row sums correctly: 3+2+1+2=8, 4+3+2+1=10, 3+2+1=6, 5+3+2+2=12; answers 8, 24, 6, 7, 42, 21, 35 recomputed. The three offered fractions are always pairwise different except when the category is exactly half (the plan's exclusion is exactly right: p/N = (N-p)/N only at half, and p/(N-p) never equals the others).
- Bar arithmetic: starter values satisfy the grain rule; rows and pixel heights are integers for every allowed (rows, step); gaps 5, 15, 20, 5 give a unique biggest; top two 65 and 55 give 120. Contradictions in the written bounds are R-78.
- Contrast ratios: confirmed except the rounding in R-79.
