## How "read" is used in this document

All web pages were fetched with a tool that returns a short summary produced by a small model, not the raw page text. "Fetched" below therefore means "the tool returned a summary I relied on", and quotations are only the short phrases the tool reported. I could not check the wording against the page itself. PDF scans came back as binary with no text. I have no PDF text extractor or OCR in this environment, so no scanned diagram or exam question was inspected. ### 1e. Own computation

A throwaway prototype in the planning agent's scratch directory (not in the repository, not a test) enumerated hexominoes and ran the folding method described in the plan. Reported results: 216 fixed hexominoes, 35 free (rotations and reflections identified), 64 fixed orientations that fold into a cube, which form 11 free nets; the 11 split into the textbook row profiles 1-4-1 (6), 2-3-1 (3), 2-2-2 (1), 3-3 (1). Valid nets fit inside 4 by 3, 3 by 4, 5 by 2 or 2 by 5. Of the pentomino pieces left by deleting one removable square from a valid net, the prototype found 45 distinct fixed pieces, each with 8 to 11 empty edge-adjacent cells of which exactly 4 complete a cube net. That last figure is a prototype observation, not a proven property; the coding agent must recompute it, not hard-code it. These agree with common knowledge (11 nets of a cube) but are my own computation, not sourced.

## 3. Task types observed

These are described in my own words from the sources above. None is copied. Several come from tuition guides describing what children meet, not from papers.

### Bar graphs (low evidence)

Sources say little. Observed by implication (S2): read a bar, solve word problems with a graph. From the line-graph precedent and the syllabus step from P3: read the scale step, read a value, compare two bars, find a difference, add some bars. Double bar graphs, a scale with half-step reading and average are not placed at P4 by any opened source; average is placed at P6 by S4 and S5. I did not see a past paper question.

### Pie charts

Work out what the whole is, then treat each sector as a fraction of the whole (S7). Convert category sizes to fractions and recover actual numbers from a known total (S10, S11). Find a missing total from one known sector (S11, S12; the harder form compares different wholes and is PSLE level). Compare which sector is larger. Fractions sharing a denominator add to one whole (search summary, unopened). Percentages and sector angles are mentioned only by later-level sources.

### Nets

Name the solid a given net makes (S6). Choose which net folds into a given solid (S6, S13). Tell valid from invalid nets (S13). Draw a net (S6, S13). Count faces first, then check the arrangement because six squares in the wrong layout fail (S6, S13). Not observed in any opened source: which face is opposite which, completing a partial net, counting faces, edges and vertices. Those are proposed in the plan as natural tasks but remain design choices, not evidence.
